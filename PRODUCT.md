# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

JiKay serves listeners, music-industry collaborators, creative clients, and Ableton Live users. The beta programme is for Ableton Live users who regularly work with Max for Live devices and want to test unreleased creative tools.

## Product Purpose

The JiKay website brings together JiKay's music, sync work, creative tools, and releases. The beta programme recruits a varied pool of testers whose feedback can improve the usability, compatibility, workflow, and overall quality of upcoming Max for Live devices.

## Positioning

JiKay combines an active music practice with the development of practical creative tools for Ableton Live users, inviting testers to influence those tools before release.

## Operating Context

The site is a static web presence. Beta applicants use Ableton Live, explore pre-release Max for Live devices, report bugs, and suggest improvements. Individual betas have limited places and participants are selected according to each project's needs.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Beta applications are collected through an embedded Google Form.
- Previous beta-testing experience is not required.
- Not every applicant will be invited to every beta.
- Active participants who complete a beta test receive a complimentary licence for the finished device.
- Applicant information is used only to manage the programme and contact applicants about relevant opportunities, and is not shared with third parties.

## Brand Commitments

Preserve the JiKay name, existing white logo, dark studio imagery, concise first-person voice, and the visual character established by the homepage. New pages should feel recognisably part of the same site.

## Evidence on Hand

The repository contains the JiKay logo, studio photograph, favicons, social-share artwork, and the approved first draft of the beta programme copy. No testimonials, participation figures, release dates, or device claims should be invented.

## Product Principles

- Keep creative work and tools personal, direct, and useful.
- Make participation expectations clear before asking someone to apply.
- Treat tester feedback as collaboration rather than promotion.
- Preserve trust through transparent selection, reward, and privacy language.
